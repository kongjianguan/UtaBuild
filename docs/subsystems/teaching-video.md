## Source preparation

[`prepare.mjs`](../../tools/teaching-video/prepare.mjs) selects the requested song from QQ Music, retrieves original and romaji QRC, calls the Rust [`teaching` module](../../cli/src/teaching.rs) to decrypt the responses and obtain UtaTen Ruby, and builds a timed project. `fast-xml-parser` reads the QRC container and `@applemusic-like-lyrics/lyric` parses the timed lyrics. The command selects a complete official recording by matching title, artist, album, and duration, then downloads it with `yt-dlp`. Album comparison uses `opencc-js` to normalize Simplified and Traditional Chinese while retaining the original metadata in the source evidence. FFprobe checks recording duration and audio-stream presence.

The project preserves original source word intervals and the paired Japanese romaji intervals. Japanese romaji must contain complete text and timing; Latin-script lines use their original source tokens and intervals for the romaji row. All source Ruby spans must map to the complete original through Unicode codepoint offsets, including whitespace. Publication punctuation is normalized during source-text matching; the project preserves the original text. Source title, artist, lyric URL, Ruby URL, cover artwork, and recording duration travel with the project. The preparation command retains raw source responses and parsed timing data so the generated project can be checked against its inputs. It writes `project-source.json` and publishes `project.json` only after automatic lyric calibration succeeds.

## Automatic lyric calibration

[`calibrate.py`](../../tools/teaching-video/calibrate.py), exposed through `pnpm video:calibrate`, measures the supplied Japanese lyrics against the selected recording with faster-whisper and CTranslate2. The default `small` model uses the known text for acoustic matching. The command divides lyric occurrences into three separate song regions. Within each region, it selects the consecutive complete lines containing the most normalized lyric text within 22 seconds, then includes up to four seconds of audio before and after them. Each model window stays within 30 seconds. Model files are cached under the ignored `work/whisper-models/` directory.

Each measurement pairs an original QRC word start with its measured audio start and known-text token probability. Token probability screens the supplied text; residuals and regional consistency test the measured offset. [`timing.py`](../../tools/teaching-video/timing.py) owns the token-probability, residual-error, sample-count, and regional-consistency criteria. Calibration estimates one common offset from accepted measurements and checks all three regions independently. Insufficient qualifying measurements, inconsistent offsets, or regional drift stop calibration.

The calibrated project requires `lyric_offset_ms` and an absolute `calibration_path`. Effective lyric time is `QRC time + lyric_offset_ms`; a positive offset delays lyric display. The shared renderer applies this offset to line, original-word, and romaji-word intervals in memory. Original QRC intervals, Ruby relationships, and the complete audio remain intact.

For `--output project.json`, calibration writes `project.calibration.json` and `project.measurements.json` beside the output project. The report records the offset, raw anchors, regional residuals, model identity and hash, and hashes of the audio and original lyric data. Completed acoustic measurements remain available when statistical acceptance fails. Project loading verifies the audio and original-data hashes, reconstructs the anchor analysis, and requires the project offset to match the accepted report.

## Teaching content

`teaching-input.json` lists unique sung lines and their source Ruby. The working Agent writes and reviews the corresponding `teaching.json`. Each entry preserves the source wording and carries its Chinese translation, contextual vocabulary, and expression explanations. The renderer matches NFC-normalized text with whitespace removed; repeated sung lines share an entry. Vocabulary surfaces and expression surfaces belong to that line; readings follow the contextual source readings, and meanings and conjugations explain the actual use.

The renderer's schema is the field authority. A vocabulary card distinguishes surface, reading, lemma, part of speech, conjugation, meaning, and JLPT level; the required `level` field uses `null` when a consulted source does not support a label. The full set of unique sung lines must be covered before rendering. Structural validation establishes completeness and field validity; the Agent's complete content review establishes language accuracy.

## Rendering

[`render.py`](../../tools/teaching-video/render.py) validates the project and teaching file, loads the explicit CJK font, and composes the frame with Pillow. The renderer sends frames to FFmpeg to encode the MP4 with the selected audio. Its command declaration owns layouts, dimensions, colors, time-range options, and audio-offset semantics.

Original text, Ruby, and romaji each use a shared font baseline within each rendered row. Glyphs retain the font's vertical offsets from that baseline. White text and its progressive highlight use the same raster masks and positions.

Vocabulary and expression cards use the Liquid Glass material implemented by [`glass.py`](../../tools/teaching-video/glass.py). The material samples the actual cover background at each card's final position, applies local magnification and blur, and composites a translucent tint, rounded edge highlights, and a soft shadow. The card's text area retains the background-brightness limit used by automatic accent selection. Material composition occurs once per teaching page; previews and MP4 frames reuse the same completed card backgrounds. Teaching-page inventories record the material identifier and corner radius.

Original and romaji highlighting use their calibrated time intervals. Ruby remains attached to its base text while the sung phrase advances. Vocabulary cards and expression explanations change with the active line. Content that requires several pages is distributed across that line's calibrated display interval. Landscape and portrait have separate geometry and column counts; a complete export covers the recording from its beginning to its end with a zero audio offset. Nonzero audio offsets apply to selected clips.

The renderer captures the project, teaching, audio, background, font, and calibration-report hashes before encoding and checks that those inputs remain unchanged when encoding finishes. It writes a `.render.json` manifest beside the MP4 with those captured input hashes, the calibration path and lyric offset, the output hash, composition settings, encoded range, frame count, source-audio offset, teaching-page inventories, and original glyph geometry. This manifest binds later verification to the exact exported inputs and file.

## Post-production editing

[`editor.py`](../../tools/teaching-video/editor.py), exposed through `pnpm video:edit`, serves the editing interface and media on the local loopback address. FastAPI validates requests, and Pillow supplies the actual rendered preview. The browser places selectable regions over that picture and provides direct text editing, pointer dragging, coordinate controls, component deletion, undo, redo, and separate orientation positions. The same [`composition.py`](../../tools/teaching-video/composition.py) constructs previews and encoded frames.

`EDIT_SCHEMA` owns the version 2 edit-document contract: display-text overrides, deleted identifiers, and hidden identifiers are shared across orientations. Position offsets, automatic-layout switches, and scene arrangement records are stored per orientation. Song-level components use stable identifiers; lyric components include the source line identifier and their parent region. Individual timed-word replacements preserve source intervals. Ruby spans map through the changed codepoint boundaries, and their displayed readings remain explicitly editable. Prepared source and teaching files retain their original contents.

Selection and operations are independent. Shift-click and marquee selection operate on the preview; scene checkboxes and component-type filters select current, marked, or all lyric occurrences. The catalog includes components on other teaching pages and hidden components. A move applies the same displacement to selected roots and their descendants; selecting a parent and child together applies the displacement once. Original-word selection links its source Ruby by default, and direct text editing selects one text field. Multi-selection, hiding, showing, deletion, and resetting offsets each form one undoable operation.

Automatic layout is disabled by default. Fixed arrangements retain unselected text positions, timed-text baselines, card rectangles, and page assignments when text, visibility, or deletion changes. Hidden components retain content and manual positions and remain selectable in the component list. Automatic layout measures visible edited text, repacks vocabulary columns, and recomputes teaching pagination throughout the current orientation. Turning it off captures the current arrangement without clearing manual offsets. One-shot layout uses the chosen scene range, clears those scenes' manual offsets in the current orientation, and captures their complete arrangement without changing the automatic-layout switch. It can clear an invalid scene displacement while preserving the failing state in undo history. This explicit scene operation covers all components in its scope, independently of component-type selection. Ordinary text wraps to the component width. The renderer rejects unavailable glyphs, invalid arrangements, components outside the picture, content beyond the available height, and pages with insufficient reading time.

Pillow quantizes the real cover artwork to select a dominant color. The renderer preserves its hue and adjusts lightness for at least 4.5:1 contrast against the brightest possible darkened cover background. This color supplies lyric, Ruby, and romaji highlighting and teaching accents. An explicit command-line accent overrides automatic selection.

The editing directory binds saved edits to the selected project, teaching file, cover, font settings, calibration report, and lyric offset. Scene seeking, previews, and exports share calibrated lyric time. The service rejects changes to its active source files or calibration report. Every export receives a separate edit snapshot, MP4, render manifest, logs, and verification evidence. The manifest captures the edit path and hash alongside the media-input hashes; verification reconstructs the edited composition and checks that snapshot. The export interface exposes downloadable artifacts only after verification succeeds.

## Failure behavior

Preparation requires a new or empty output directory and stops when that directory contains files, the song cannot be identified, required lyric or reading sources are missing, source text cannot be matched, romaji timing is absent, the recording duration differs from the selected song, or automatic calibration fails. Calibration requires an independent unused output project and evidence paths. Rendering stops on an invalid project or teaching file, missing or inconsistent calibration evidence, calibrated sung intervals outside the recording, incomplete line coverage, unavailable glyphs, a layout that cannot contain the content, insufficient display time for teaching pages, a nonzero audio offset for the full recording, an existing output file, inputs changing during encoding, or an encoder failure. These failures require correcting the responsible source, content, or output parameters before exporting again.

## Verification

[`verify.py`](../../tools/teaching-video/verify.py), exposed through `pnpm video:verify`, verifies the MP4 against its render manifest and input hashes. It checks the calibration-report hash and lyric offset against the manifest, repeats the three-region anchor analysis, and samples the picture at calibrated lyric times. `--require-full-song` requires a zero start time, the frame count for the complete recording, and a zero audio offset. The verifier decodes the full file, checks stream metadata, compares encoded samples from every displayed teaching page and selected highlight moments with the expected composition, and compares the exported audio samples with the selected source audio and offset. For sampled original characters, it independently reconstructs the font's actual raster at the recorded row baseline and checks the glyph position in the encoded frame. The report includes glyph sample counts and measured vertical offsets.

The evidence directory contains `verification.json` and the sampled frame files. Its `lyric_alignment` section preserves the accepted calibration report, offset, path, and hash. The report distinguishes complete recordings with `song_scope: full` from selected intervals with `song_scope: clip`; its scene and page counts cover the actual encoded range. A complete-song deliverable requires both `status: passed` and `song_scope: full`.

Changes to shared lyric behavior also follow the [core lyric pipeline checks](../../AGENTS.md) and inspect the complete fixture JSON. The [user tutorial](../user/teaching-video.md) owns the execution sequence, and the [project Skill](../../.agents/skills/generate-teaching-video/SKILL.md) owns the reusable Agent workflow.
